# Technology Stack & Architecture Standards

## Introduction

Technology decisions have long-term consequences.

Every technology adopted by RIN shall improve reliability, maintainability, security, and engineering quality.

Technology selection is guided by engineering principles rather than popularity.

---

# Engineering Law 1

## Production Before Popularity

Technology shall be selected based on engineering value rather than current industry trends.

---

# Engineering Law 2

## Long-Term Support

Official technologies should provide long-term maintenance, stable ecosystems, and active community support.

---

# Engineering Law 3

## Cross-Platform Engineering

Whenever practical, technologies should support both Windows and Android while remaining adaptable to future platforms.

---

# Engineering Law 4

## Modular Architecture

Every major component should remain modular and independently maintainable.

Technology choices should encourage separation of concerns.

---

# Engineering Law 5

## Open Standards

Whenever practical, RIN shall adopt open standards and well-documented technologies to improve interoperability and future evolution.

---

# Best Practices

- Prefer stable technologies over experimental frameworks.
- Minimize unnecessary dependencies.
- Keep the technology stack consistent.
- Review technology decisions periodically.
- Document every significant technology adoption.

---

# Common Mistakes

Avoid:

- Technology selection based solely on popularity.
- Introducing multiple frameworks for the same responsibility.
- Frequent technology migrations without engineering justification.
- Unnecessary dependencies that increase maintenance complexity.

---

# Engineering Checklist

Before adopting a new technology:

- Does it improve engineering quality?
- Is it production-ready?
- Is it actively maintained?
- Is it secure?
- Is it compatible with the existing architecture?
- Does it reduce long-term technical debt?

If any answer is **No**, adoption should be reconsidered.

---

# Official Constitution

> "Technology shall always serve engineering. Engineering shall never become a servant of technology."
