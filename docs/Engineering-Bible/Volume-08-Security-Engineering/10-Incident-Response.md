# Incident Response

## Introduction

The Incident Response architecture defines how the RIN ecosystem detects, analyzes, contains, recovers from, and learns from security and operational incidents.

No engineering system can guarantee the complete absence of incidents.

A production-grade system is measured by how responsibly, transparently, and effectively it responds when incidents occur.

---

# Purpose

The purpose of Incident Response is to minimize the impact of security and operational incidents while preserving the integrity, availability, privacy, and trustworthiness of the RIN ecosystem.

Every incident shall contribute to continuous engineering improvement.

---

# Responsibilities

The Incident Response System is responsible for:

- Incident Detection
- Incident Classification
- Incident Containment
- Incident Recovery
- Evidence Preservation
- Communication
- Root Cause Analysis
- Continuous Improvement

# Incident Categories

## Security Incident

Examples:

- Unauthorized access
- Credential compromise
- Permission bypass attempt
- Plugin security violation

---

## Runtime Incident

Examples:

- Core failure
- Memory corruption
- Service outage
- Critical subsystem crash

---

## Data Incident

Examples:

- Unexpected data loss
- Backup failure

## Communication Incident

# Response Principles

## Rapid Detection

Potential incidents shall be identified as early as practical.

---

## Containment Before Recovery

Prevent additional damage before restoring normal operation.

---

## Evidence Preservation

Relevant diagnostic and audit information shall remain available for investigation.

---

## Controlled Recovery

Recovery actions shall prioritize integrity over speed.

---

## Continuous Learning

Every confirmed incident shall improve future engineering practices.

---

# Engineering Laws

## Law 1

Every critical incident shall be classified.

---

## Law 2

Incident containment shall begin before recovery.

---

## Law 3

Evidence shall remain protected.

---

## Law 4

Recovery shall be verified before normal operation resumes.

---

## Law 5

Every major incident shall result in documented engineering improvements.

---

## Law 6

Incident handling shall preserve the trust of the Primary Owner.

---

# Best Practices

- Detect incidents early.
- Preserve evidence.
- Communicate clearly.
- Validate recovery.
- Review root causes objectively.

---

# Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- Ignoring warning signs.
- Destroying diagnostic evidence.
- Rushing recovery without verification.
- Hiding incidents.
- Repeating unresolved engineering failures.

---

# Engineering Checklist

Before closing an incident:

- Incident classified.
- Containment completed.
- Recovery verified.
- Root cause documented.
- Engineering improvements identified.

---

Future improvements shall strengthen resilience while preserving transparency and engineering discipline.
